# Iris native 40-step recipe comparison

The reviewed 40-step rank-density comparison favored Scotch48 over Scotch56 by 17.38%; a reverse-order confirmation passed root and independent review with a 19.21% reduction. That makes a 48-rank recipe follow-up distinct and testable, not a settled speed claim. Historical Iris results recorded teammate-style 22.39695 and 27.09361 seconds per step (pooled mean 24.74528) and organizer-style 25.58196 and 30.16498. The 22.39695 result is a reference point, not a record declared broken: this trial directly measures teammate56, original48, and hybrid48 under current matched conditions. The prior and current runs share Iris node iris001, binary hashes, library identities, and N56 source manifest; no cause of their variation is established. This next trial tests whether pairing 48 ranks with the teammate launch recipe improves absolute solver time. Six arms use this order: teammate56, original48, hybrid48, hybrid48, original48, teammate56. The hybrid applies the teammate MPI `--map-by socket --bind-to core` flags and `-fileHandler collated` to the accepted 48-rank source. The controls are teammate56 with those flags and collated I/O, and our 48-rank default mapping with default uncollated I/O. This tests bundled recipes; it does not isolate an individual flag effect.

Each arm retains the same 40-step horizon and 39 timed intervals, fixed-iteration settings, OpenFOAM v2512 double-precision runtime, initialized rank-specific source, 56-CPU exclusive Iris node, and preparation/checks. Matching launch probes use the recipe's MPI mapping flags. Solver output must confirm the expected file handler. Same-recipe repeats use the existing tolerance. Original48 and hybrid48 also share the same 48-rank Scotch source; their paired force-series drift is recorded and checked with that tolerance as an unchanged-partition regression guard. No cross-decomposition Cd equality gate is applied to teammate56 versus 48-rank arms. Results retain every step timing and force row and report per-recipe mean seconds per step, hybrid-minus-control seconds, and percentages. No historical 22-second record is claimed broken unless measured values support that statement.

Resource bounds: 56 allocated CPUs, zero GPUs, low QoS, one node, four-hour Slurm maximum (224 allocated CPU-hours), six solver caps of 1,800 seconds, six preparation caps of 150 seconds, a 480-second finalization reserve, and a 13,900-second internal deadline. The runner requires 96 GiB currently free on the payload filesystem for six isolated case copies.

This is a native short-horizon CPU comparison, not a full solver result, contest score, or promotion by itself. Launcher reports and pre-MPI_Init affinity probes do not prove post-MPI helper-thread affinity or NUMA page residency.
